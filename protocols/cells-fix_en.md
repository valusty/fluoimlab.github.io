# Preparation
## 16% PFA Stock Solution

**Safety Warning:** Paraformaldehyde is toxic, and a suspected carcinogen. Always work inside a certified fume hood. Wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including gloves, lab coat, and safety goggles/glasses. Do not inhale the powder or vapors.

[**Video guide**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ntDN5a9-vWk)

### Materials:
| Name | For 100 mL |
| :--- | :--- |
| Paraformaldehyde (PFA) powder | 16 g (Sigma-Aldrich, catalog number: P6148) |
| Deionized or distilled water (ddH₂O) | ~100 mL |
| 1N (or 1M) Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) | ~1-5 drops :) |
| Hot plate with magnetic stirrer | - |
| Magnetic stir bar | - |
| Glass beaker | V=150mL |
| Thermometer | - |
| Filter paper and funnel/syringe filter | e.g., 0.45 µm |
| pH meter (optional) | - |

### Protocol (for 100 mL):
1. **Preparation:** Measure **80 mL** of deionized water (ddH₂O) into a glass beaker. Add a magnetic stir bar and place it on the hot plate inside the fume hood. Heating: Heat the water to approximately **60°C***.
   *\*Note: Do not allow the temperature to exceed 65-70°C, and never let it boil, as this degrades the formaldehyde and releases dangerous vapors.*
2. **Dissolving:** Weigh 16 g of Paraformaldehyde powder. Add the powder to the warm water while stirring. The solution will become very cloudy (milky). The powder will not dissolve immediately.
3. **Clearing (Depolymerization):** While stirring at **60°C**, slowly add 1N NaOH dropwise. Add 1 drop, wait a minute, then add another if needed. Usually, only a small amount (**1-5 drops**) is required.
4. The solution should turn clear within a few minutes as the solution becomes slightly alkaline. Once clear, stop adding NaOH immediately.
5. **Cooling and Filtering:** Remove the beaker from the heat and allow the solution to cool to room temperature.
6. Filter the solution to remove any undissolved particles.
7. **Final Volume Adjustment:** Transfer the solution to a graduated cylinder (or volumetric flask) and add deionized water to bring the total volume to **100 mL**.
8. **pH Adjustment (Optional):** Check the pH. It usually settles around neutral (pH **7.0–7.4**). If precise pH is critical for your stock, adjust carefully with diluted HCl, but this is often done later when diluting to the working concentration (e.g., 4% in PBS).

### Storage:
Aliquot the 16% stock solution into small tubes (e.g., 10 mL or 50 mL tubes). Store at -20°C for long-term storage (months/years). Thaw aliquots as needed. Once thawed, keep at 4°C and use within a few days to a week (avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles).

### Optimized dilution protocol (4%):
Simply adding 16% formaldehyde solution to regular 1x PBS is not entirely correct for delicate cells (such as neurons). This will lower the salt concentration, and the cells may swell.

#### Method 1: To preserve ideal morphology
For this, you will need concentrated 10x PBS and water.

**Formula:**
- 1 part 16% PFA
- 0.4 parts 10x PBS
- 2.6 parts water (ddH₂O)

**Example calculation for 10 ml of the final solution:**
- 2.5 ml — 16% PFA (your stock)
- 1.0 ml — 10x PBS (so that the final salt concentration is 1x)
- 6.5 ml — Distilled water

This preserves physiological osmolarity (~300 mOsm).

#### Method 2: Simplified (if only regular 1x PBS is available)
If you do not have 10x PBS, you can use regular PBS, but the salt concentration will be slightly lower than normal (0.75x PBS).

**Formula (1:4 dilution):**
- 1 part 16% PFA
- 3 parts 1x PBS

**Example for 10 ml solution:**
- 2.5 ml — 16% PFA
- 7.5 ml — 1x PBS

*Downside: The solution will be hypotonic. For routine histology, this is often okay, but for thin membrane structures, it can create artifacts.*

# Cells fixation
For cell fixation of any type of cells and in each case of imaging (after transfection/AB/fluorescence staining) you will need:

| Name | Concentration | V |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formaldehyde** | **4%** | **1000 ul/glass (in case you use 12 well plate or 35mm Petri dish)** |
| **Sucrose** | **4g/10ml Formaldehyde 4%** | **Calculate and add sucrose to your V of Formaldehyde** |
| **1x PBS** | **-** | **1 mL/glass** |
| **H2Odist** | **-** | **-** |
| **Mounting Media (if you use slides)** | **-** | **1 drop/glass (VECTASHIELD Antifade Mounting Medium, Vector Laboratories, cat. #: H-1000-10, H-1200-10 with DAPI)** |

| Name | Note |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pipette or vacuum pump (optional)** | **1000ul + tips** |
| **Parafilm piece or Petri dish** | **1/glass** |
| **Slide (optional*)** | **1/glass** |
| **Tweezers** | **-** |
| **Marker** | **-** |
| **Gloves** | **-** |
| **Nail Polish (optional*)** | **any type, except gel** |

*\*Note: If you want to use antifade medium/AB/nanoparticles etc. you should not put your glass on the slide. Just make everything in the Petri dish.*

Glass with cells was replaced from a 12-well plate (if it was in a Petri dish - it's okay to start fixation), before the start - remove ½ medium from a well.

### Procedure:
1. For each glass, add 500ul of formaldehyde 4% with sucrose to the rest of the medium in the well, keep it in incubator for 10' \*\*
2. After that, remove all solution from the well and add 500 ul of formaldehyde 4% with sucrose, keep it in incubator for 10'
3. Remove\*\*\* formaldehyde solution;
4. Add 500 ul of PBS, leave it for 5';
5. Remove PBS and add H2Odist;
6. Name the slide/Petri dish with: cell type, transfection/AB, date;
7. If you are using a slide: drop Mounting Medium on the slide (little drop). Remove H2Odist and put glasses on the slide with revert conformation;
8. After 5', apply some nail polish on the edges of glass on the slide (see image 1);
9. Store at +4C\*\*\*\*.

*\*Note: This step is adapted for primary neuronal culture to prevent rapid vacuolization; If you are using a less demanding culture, you can add the entire volume of formaldehyde 4% with sucrose to the well, after removing all the medium and leaving it in the incubator for 15';*

*\*\*Note: Could be prepared in the Petri dish or on the surface of parafilm piece;*

*\*\*\*Note: For more efficiency, it is recommended to use a vacuum pump;*

*\*\*\*\*Note: It's better to store slides in a special box for slides.*

# Immunocytochemistry assay

| Name | Note |
| :--- | :--- |
| Permeabilization Buffer | 0.1% to 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS |
| Blocking Buffer | **5% BSA** or 5% Normal Serum (from the same species as your secondary antibody, e.g., Donkey or Goat serum) in PBS |
| Antibodies | Primary AB and Fluorophore-conjugated Secondary AB |
| Nuclear Stain (optional) | DAPI or Hoechst 33342 (could be in Mounting media) |
| Mounting Medium | VECTASHIELD, Fluoromount-G, ProLong Gold etc. |

### Procedure:
1. **Permeabilization**
   - Add 0.1%-0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS (with neurons/U83 cells = 0.1%);
   - Incubate for 10 minutes at room temperature;
   - Wash cells 3 times with PBS (5 min each);
   *Tip: Skip or shorten this step to avoid destroying the membrane structure if membrane-bound surface proteins are used.*
2. **Blocking**
   - Add Blocking Buffer (e.g., 5% BSA in PBS);
   - Incubate for 30–60 minutes at room temperature;
   *Purpose: This blocks unspecific binding sites to reduce background noise.*
3. **Primary Antibody Incubation**
   - Dilute your Primary Antibody in the Blocking Buffer according to the manufacturer's datasheet (usually 1:100 to 1:1000);
   - **Option A (Best for specific binding):** Incubate overnight at 4°C in a humidified chamber;
   - **Option B (Faster):** Incubate for 1–2 hours at room temperature;
   - After incubation, aspirate the antibody solution;
   - Wash cells 3 times with PBS (5–10 min each) to remove unbound antibodies.
4. **Secondary Antibody Incubation**
   - From this step onward, keep samples in the dark;
   - Dilute the Secondary Antibody in Blocking Buffer (typically 1:500 or 1:1000);
   - Incubate for 1 hour at room temperature in the dark;
   - Wash cells 3 times with PBS (5–10 min each).
5. **Counterstaining (Nuclei) [optional]**
   - Add DAPI (0.1–1 µg/mL in PBS) for 1–5 minutes;
   - Rinse quickly with PBS;
   *Note: Many mounting media already contain DAPI, in which case you can skip this step.*
6. **Mounting**
   - Add a drop of Mounting Medium onto a microscope slide;
   - Carefully pick up the coverslip with forceps;
   - Invert the coverslip (cells facing down) onto the drop of the mounting medium;
   *Avoid air bubbles. Allow to dry overnight in the dark before imaging.*
